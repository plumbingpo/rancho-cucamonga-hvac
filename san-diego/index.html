When Your System Fails in San Diego

If your heat or air quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls usually get a technician within a day, and we prioritize no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. Year-round, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, plugged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls get fixed in one visit, at a price you approve before we start.

Two situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a far bigger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in San Diego

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Change your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of the year.

Small problems don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.
